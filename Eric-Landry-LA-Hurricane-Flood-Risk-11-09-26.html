Parish-level storm risk for the Louisiana parishes where your leads and your office sit, from the FEMA National Risk Index (version 1.20, December 2025): the expected annual loss in dollars for hurricane, inland (river) flooding, coastal flooding, tornado, hail and strong wind. In Louisiana the first two decide the home conversation. Statewide, hurricane accounts for about $1.5 billion of roughly $2.6 billion in yearly building loss and inland flooding for another $875.6 million; hail is about $29.7 million, around 1%. Your home market makes the point: in Terrebonne Parish, where the Houma office sits, hurricane is 63% of the parish's $141 million expected annual loss, and in Lafourche (Thibodaux) it is 65%. Farther north, in East Baton Rouge, Caddo, Ouachita, Bossier and Rapides, inland flooding leads and hail and strong wind are a real but smaller part of the picture. Section 04 is the recommended treatment for Home leads, section 05 covers Houma and Thibodaux, and section 06 matches the exposure types against the 128 GOAL Auto leads delivered from October 14 to November 9, 2026. All 128 leads so far are Auto, so this report is the guide for the Home and bundle conversation as Home leads arrive. The parish-by-parish figures ship as a spreadsheet with this report.

03

How the risk is measured

Public federal data at county (parish) resolution

What goes into the figures
  • Expected annual lossNRI expected annual loss: how often a hazard strikes, times the building, population and crop value exposed, times the typical share of that value lost. It is an annualized dollar figure per hazard for every county (parish) in the country.
  • HurricaneHurricane: tropical cyclone risk as modeled in the NRI. Coastal flooding is listed as a separate hazard.
  • FloodingRiverine (inland) flooding and coastal flooding are separate NRI hazards. Flood, coastal or inland, is excluded from a standard home policy and is quoted separately (section 07).
  • Hail and windHail and strong wind (damaging non-hurricane wind) are separate hazards. Tornado is shown too, because it is the next-largest wind peril in most parishes.
  • Parish, not propertyThese are parish totals. Carrier models (roof age and material, elevation, distance to the coast) and the flood zone of the address are property-level and are not published by ZIP or parish.
  • Dollars, not labelsWe show dollars rather than NRI rating labels: the two republished sources we checked agreed on the dollar figures to within rounding, but their score and rating labels differed.

04

Home leads: recommended treatment

Hurricane-first parishes get a wind and deductible conversation · flood-first parishes get a flood-zone conversation

Home treatment by exposure type
  • Hurricane firstWind first, flood second. Lafayette, Terrebonne, Ascension, Tangipahoa, Lafourche. Ask the roof's age, shape and material, whether the home has storm shutters or roof tie-downs, and how a hurricane or wind deductible works on the policy, often a percentage of the dwelling amount. Then offer a flood quote at the first call: coastal and bayou parishes carry both.
  • Flood firstFlood first, hail and wind second. East Baton Rouge, Caddo, Ouachita, Bossier, Rapides. Ask whether the home sits in a mapped flood zone and whether it has had water in a past storm, and offer a flood quote at the first call. In Caddo, Bossier and Ouachita add the roof's age and material and the hail and wind deductible.
  • Not profiledFollow the nearest profile. 51 of your 128 leads are in parishes this edition does not profile (St. Landry, Livingston, Calcasieu and others). Treat Gulf-coast and bayou parishes as hurricane first and inland parishes as flood first until their figures are added.
  • TargetingNo change recommended. We do not recommend excluding any parish. Treat the exposure type as a conversation prompt, not a reason to turn business away; the carrier decides what it can write at the address.

07

Hurricane, flood and hail questions

Questions that make a Louisiana home quote faster and stickier

  • Flood is separateThe Louisiana Department of Insurance states that a standard homeowner's or renter's policy does not cover damage from a flood. Flood insurance is available through the National Flood Insurance Program and from private insurers, and Louisiana property and casualty agents can sell it. Offer a flood quote at the first Home call in every parish.
  • The 30-day waitNFIP policies take effect after a 30-day waiting period (Louisiana Department of Insurance), so the time to talk flood is before a storm is in the Gulf, not after.
  • LendersHomeowners with federally backed mortgages in designated flood plains may be required to carry an NFIP policy, so ask about the mortgage and the flood zone.
  • Wind or hurricane deductibleAsk how wind and hurricane damage is written on the customer's current policy and whether the deductible is a flat amount or a percentage of the dwelling. In hurricane-first parishes explain it before the first storm of the season.
  • Roof and constructionRoof age, shape and material, shutters or tie-downs, and the age of the home. In Caddo, Bossier and Ouachita add hail and impact-resistance questions.
  • SeasonThe Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30. A Business Profile post and a short customer note each May get the wind and flood conversation started.
  • Auto and stormsReview comprehensive and collision deductibles on each vehicle before storm season; the same call is a natural place to ask about the home.
